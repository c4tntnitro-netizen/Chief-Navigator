# Adept of the Stars and Waves

**Naming update:** the black-hole system formerly called Messina is now **Avici**. Historical `MESSINA_*` code symbols and persistent IDs remain unchanged solely for save compatibility; every older narrative reference to Messina below means Avici.

**Encounter naming update:** Charybdis is now **Budai**, and Scylla is now **Gautama**. Their historical code symbols, hull IDs, and persistent fleet IDs remain unchanged solely for save compatibility.

**Encounter roster update:** Budai is a lone boss before and after Labor V, without post-endgame escort growth. Ungaikyo starts with five escorts—one Hive, Overseer, Line, Assault and Skirmish—with no escort Fabricators. This supersedes the larger rosters described below. Summoned reflections and ordinary Strike fleets are unchanged; already-generated saved rosters are not rebuilt.

**Avici population update:** Avici has no Starving Threat patrols or failed-Labor gate incursions. Retire existing owned local Starving fleets when safe, preserving foreign/player fleets, Budai, and the canonical story boss's existing relocation path. Budai's vignette gate, lone roster, pursuit, music and rewards are unchanged; other systems retain their populations.

**Budai leap timing update:** Budai uses the regular Maw's complete `convulsive_lunge` timing: two seconds charging, three active, half a second winding down, and a twelve-second cooldown after every leap. This restores the full 5.5-second cycle instead of his former shortened 2.1-second cycle. Every leap respects activation readiness and any live remaining cooldown. The half-hull sequence still chooses four random destinations without clearing cooldown between leaps or after the fourth; its bounded timeout is ninety seconds so full cycles and existing/modifier cooldowns do not prematurely skip the fourth leap. Ordinary backward escape lunges are removed; native offensive targeting and the separate Hungering Rift enrage buff remain unchanged. Budai holds his ground even while venting, with native do-not-back-off flags.

**Budai health and music update:** Budai has no passive HP regeneration; the finite healing reserve is removed. His maximum hull is 30% lower than the previous boss, using a 1.4x vanilla Maw hull multiplier instead of 2x. His music still switches irreversibly to **The Savor of Tomorrow** strictly below 50% HP, while enrage effects trigger at or below half. His bubble uses the `DEFLECTED!` hit message. Movement, lone roster, twelve-second leap cooldown and rewards remain unchanged.

**Post-Labor V hunts:** finishing Menelaus's final debrief starts separate Hunt Budai and Hunt Ungaikyo missions. ## Firm decisions

- The canonical title of the entire Orion Knot story is **Adept of the Stars and Waves**. **Chief Navigator** remains the standalone mod/project name.

### Quest hook

- Deploying the Mara sensor package completes only Labor IV's field objective. Its Intel changes to **Return to Menelaus**; Labor V remains inactive after the player reports at Ithaca and reads Menelaus's warning. It starts only when the player explicitly selects the red **Accept the final Labor** option. That selection creates the Labor V Intel entry, starts the three perimeter invasions, and begins their instrumental **My Gospel is Gunpowder** cue.
- Menelaus's defense-upgrade ledger identifies the exact system and planetary orbit of the research station containing every component that has not yet been recovered.
- Sinni's live **where to go next** guidance treats Mara as Labor IV's primary destination. Before the TTS Penumbra is recovered, she may mention its optional Doom-class recovery lead in Sanzu; afterward, she recommends bringing that phase ship for the Mara survey-communications deployment. Once four Ithaca components are installed, she may likewise point out any remaining research stations and components as optional without reopening Labor II.

- **Sinni begins appearing in bars only after the player reaches the maximum standard level of Hyperspace Topography.**
- Once that prerequisite is met, the extraordinarily skilled smuggler and navigator approaches the player with a proposal. Recruiting her creates the top-level **Adept of the Stars and Waves** Intel entry with an invitation to take tea at her Eventide office, alongside the **One More Horizon** sightseeing quest, without revealing the route. During that private meeting she explains the Hegemony expedition and Waypoint Troy; the persistent story Intel advances to Troy and the navigation marker appears only after the tea briefing is complete.
- The player supplies an expedition fleet; Sinni supplies the route and assumes navigational leadership for the crossing.
- Once recruited, Sinni remains a permanent callable contact as well as an officer. Her contact channel identifies the current quest destination and gives usable text directions from the fleet's live position. Only after arriving in the Orion Knot, the contact can launch a dedicated full-size chart menu of its six systems without opening or creating another Intel entry, so the player can choose either the chart or plain-language navigation.
- The route is a once-in-a-lifetime alignment of slipstreams, hyperspace storms, and Abyssal conditions.
- Sinni is a competent master navigator, not a cheap betrayal twist.

#### Helen's record and Sinni's return

- Sinni is the descendant of **Helen**, a female member of Task Force Spartan who served at FOB Ithaca during the Fourteenth Battlegroup remnant's years there.
- During those years, the Fourteenth attempted to reestablish control over its sector and contact the Domain heartland. Menelaus fell in love with Helen while she lived and served at Ithaca.
- Helen would talk with Menelaus about the Persean Sector as an exciting new frontier of the Domain. She was the person who treated him most like a human.
- Dwindling supplies eventually forced the main Fourteenth remnant to abandon Task Force Spartan and attempt an Abyssal crossing to the Persean Sector. Menelaus reclassified Helen's navigation specialty, released her transfer after her shuttle joined the departure column, and held her appeal until she could no longer return. He denied her choice to remain with Spartan because he loved her. Her recovered transmission condemns the act; Menelaus acknowledges that his love gave him no authority over her choice.
- Helen wrote an account of the journey from FOB Ithaca to the Persean Sector. Her record survived through the family descended from her.
- Sinni inherits Helen's account, reconstructs the route in reverse, and uses it to lead the player's expedition back across the Abyss to Ithaca. Her connection to Menelaus is historical and familial only through Helen: Sinni is not his daughter, creation, clone, or engineered heir.
- After Labor V, Sinni surmises Menelaus's motive before he admits it. She then shares Helen's later history: navigating the survivors through the Abyss, surveying Persean supply routes, leaving the Navy for civilian freight work, unsuccessfully proposing a return expedition, marrying a drive technician from the crossing, and raising the daughter who became Sinni's grandmother. Helen died at home of heart disease, with her daughter and granddaughter nearby. Sinni never met her and cannot say whether she forgave Menelaus. The scene ends with Sinni sharing the family archive and identifying its photographs for him. A separate completion flag keeps this continuation available to saves that have already heard the original confession.
- Menelaus believes himself to be human. He was created from human data and does not regard himself as a machine merely imitating humanity; in his own words, he is arguably the spirit of humanity's **zeitgeist**. The narrative should preserve this as his sincere self-understanding without settling whether that claim is philosophically true.
- Boaz is not a character. Sinni has no hopeful, bitter, cynical, or reconciliation route; she follows one authored character arc, although individual player responses may still vary in tone.

#### Last Light and the shelved Omega prototype

- The Time Freeze/Omega boss is shelved. Its implementation and original procedural audio remain dormant for possible later reuse, but it does not spawn; an exact owned prototype fleet already serialized in an older save is retired safely.
- A battered Guardian droneship lies derelict in orbit around Ember. It remains visible but cannot be recovered until the player defeats Ungaikyo's post-Labor V Third Strike in Last Light. The Guardian retains the vanilla ship's appearance, performance, and standard loadout; the authored wreck only removes the normal recovery prohibition. The one-time wreck is added to existing saves and never respawns after recovery.
- After Sinni is recruited, immediately add and mark **One More Horizon**, her sightseeing quest, and give her a one-time comment for each of seven broad star-system categories: Nebula, Black Hole, Neutron Star, White Dwarf, Main Sequence, Giant or Massive Star, and Trinary Star System. Its advertised completion reward is +2 Burn speed cap and +1 base Burn level. Brown dwarfs are not a required category, though her completed personal chart still lists one; giant and massive stars share one chart entry while retaining different scene variants. The trinary scene has Sinni replace Enya's Domain-standard three-body route with her own propagator, feeding relativistic corrections back into each Navier-Stokes-Garcia fluid-evolution step before chaotic error can compound into dangerous stellar turbulence. Queue an unseen category on entry, wait while a hostile fleet in that system is actively tracking the player, and open it on the first clear campaign frame. A nearby hostile that is not pursuing or maintaining contact does not block the scene. Core systems, the Orion Knot, Gan Eden, and its Power Transit Gate system are excluded from generic exploration vignettes. Persist completion only when the conversation reaches its final Continue option, never merely when the scene opens. The Troy Terminus crossing additionally owns a bespoke one-time Alpha Odyssey arrival passage; it takes priority over the generic Nebula vignette without consuming that stellar category. There is no separate one-category-left detour request. Once every category available somewhere in the eligible generated Sector is complete, queue the existing chart-completion epilogue for one full campaign day after the last category's closing choice. Persist its queue timestamp across save/load; older pending saves without a timestamp begin a fresh one-day delay. Categories absent from the entire eligible Sector are omitted from the census so procgen can never make completion impossible. Retire old request state and its waypoint without changing earned progress or rewards. The epilogue's hug response is an unadvertised secret shown only after the player recovers the Devoured Ring's information about a path toward the Domain heartland.

#### Hyperspace Topography progression

- Recruiting Sinni unlocks a new set of tiered Hyperspace Topography levels beyond the standard progression. Advancing through these tiers unlocks increasingly powerful hyperspace-navigation features.
- At 1,050 points, **Slipsurge Attunement** allows **Generate Slipsurge** to be activated from any gravity well, not only the most powerful ones.
- At 1,200 points, **Ambush Stance** is unlocked. While it is active, engaging a hostile fleet from within a nebula, asteroid field, or any other terrain that reduces the player's sensor range or detectability offers the option to force a pursuit battle, as if the enemy fleet were attempting to retreat.
- At 1,350 points, **Slipsurge Mastery** makes generated slipsurges twice as wide and three times as long.
- At 1,550 points, the final permanent tier, **Storm Rider** reduces combat-readiness damage from hyperspace storms by 50%.
- At 1,750 points, **Echo Mapping** makes each manual **Active Sensor Burst** deploy a temporary Remnant-style warning beacon at the activation point. The beacon remains fixed and refires the burst after 3, 6, and 9 days. Each sensor ring, contact reveal, and hostile response is centered on the beacon rather than the moving player fleet; nearby enemies investigate the beacon, and it disappears after the sequence ends.
- At 1,950 points, the repeatable topographic-data payout is awarded and progress resets to 1,750.

### Troy and Ithaca

- After the expedition arrives, Sinni's contact opens a full-size **Orion Knot Sector** chart that normalizes the six authored systems' relative campaign coordinates into its own panel. It draws the chart and local player marker independently of the vanilla sector map, whose viewport cannot reach the Knot's remote hyperspace coordinates. The quest Intel does not embed another map.
- **Waypoint Troy** is not a permanent Domain installation. It is the departure location Sinni predicts for the temporary route, serviced by a small cooperative expedition anchorage where the player can leave ships and cargo behind.
- The anchorage is the route's manifest checkpoint. The expedition cannot cross with more than **30 deployment points** of active ships in either direction. The restriction is checked when Sinni opens the route, when the fleet enters the Troy-side wormhole, and again at **Foxtrot Terminus Aperture**, the Alpha Odyssey endpoint. Ships recovered inside the **Odyssey Sector** therefore have to be stored at Ithaca or consolidated into a 30-DP return manifest before the expedition can leave.
- Passing the anchorage check opens **Troy Terminus**, an interactable wormhole waypoint that carries the expedition into the Odyssey Expanse.
- The destination is **FOB Ithaca**, located within **Ashen Verge**.
- FOB Ithaca's central station is deliberately not a market. A custom colony-style interaction exposes fleet, persistent cargo storage, refit, and a portrait comm directory without letting vanilla market population logic create extra NPCs. Menelaus's directory entry provides free full-fleet hull, armor, and combat-readiness repairs and contains the complete Labors conversation; Sinni remains aboard and is not listed as an Ithaca contact. This is the expedition's internal logistics base and the place to retain recovered hulls that cannot fit through Foxtrot Terminus Aperture's 30-DP limit.
- Ithaca is a planet-scale military macrobase: roughly three orders of magnitude larger than even the largest conventional stations, but smaller than a megastructure such as Gan Eden and no larger than a Dyson-sphere scale construct.
- Ithaca is tied to the Fourteenth Battlegroup's history. After the Collapse, the stranded Fourteenth ran short of supplies because the FOB was not intended for sustained habitation. They departed for the Persean Sector, arriving starved and battered yet still powerful enough to impose the Hegemony over nearly the entire Sector.
- The scale of Ithaca and its abandoned materiel should reflect how overwhelmingly powerful the Fourteenth was even after that catastrophic journey.

### The crossing

- Sparse hostile **Rogue Combat Guard Flotillas** roam the Odyssey Expanse, Ashen Verge, and Last Light. All authored mobile Derelict fleets use teal Combat Guard variants instead of stock-colored Derelict hulls, including migration of fleets already serialized in a save. They are one-time encounters and never respawn after defeat. Their fixed anchors remain well clear of the Guard Mothership Labor sites, and first creation is deferred whenever the player is within 7,000 units so a hostile fleet can never materialize on top of them. Sanzu has no active Guard or Rogue patrols. Guard flotillas and the other authored autonomous Derelict fleets cannot jump and never enter hyperspace. All authored mobile Derelict patrol families use the campaign navigation layer to bend their existing routes and pursuits away from every in-system jump point. A patrol entering a jump's 4,500-unit arrival danger radius is moved intact beyond 6,500 units from every overlapping exclusion zone without replacing the fleet or changing its orders, while Rogue patrol anchors remain at least 7,500 units away. Disabled Combat Guard hulls remain eligible for ordinary post-battle recovery, making these finite encounters an important source of replacement ships while the expedition is isolated beyond Troy Terminus.
- Six one-use vanilla-style derelict research stations orbit authored worlds across the Odyssey Expanse, Sanzu, and Ashen Verge, two per system. Each suppresses all AI-core salvage and instead drops one unique special component for a specific Ithaca defense upgrade: Colossal Traverse Macroservos, Naval Cycling Regulator, Caged-Arc Projector, Interception Command Matrix, Siege Munitions Compiler, or Fortress Autoloader Core. Components remain inert cargo until Menelaus consumes and installs them at FOB Ithaca; his ledger tracks installed, recovered-awaiting-installation, and missing parts. Upgrade availability comes from the installed component set, never an ordinal station-count level. Labor II requires delivery and installation of any four distinct components, while all six stations and components remain individually tracked and recoverable and the final two are optional. Existing saves receive the matching items for previously salvaged stations. The Interception Command Matrix activates four fixed Cloudswarm SRM launchers, each with a replenishing 41-missile PD burst; the Fortress Autoloader Core expands each burst and magazine to 81 while applying its faster missile cycling. Ithaca torpedo batteries use Hammers without research; the Siege Munitions Compiler converts them to guided Atropos torpedoes while preserving the fortress battery's salvo and range. If Isa Leicester has ever been recruited as an officer, copy Hall of Triumph's durable recruitment flag into Chief Navigator save state and permanently enable her naval fire-control package: the original beam deals 50% more shield damage and shield contact emits one half-strength secondary beam to another enemy, with no recursive propagation. Derelict mining stations and orbital habitats likewise occupy curated planetary orbits instead of arbitrary free-floating coordinates. These structures migrate into their authored orbits in existing saves while preserving stable IDs, and none regenerate after being salvaged.

- The expedition must travel through slipstreams and Abyssal hyperspace for an immense distance.
- The crossing tests navigation skill, supply endurance, and expedition fleet composition.
- It includes at least one THREAT encounter and at least one Shrouded Dweller encounter.
- The expedition encounters both **Gautama and Budai**, but THREAT fleets remain confined to their authored realspace systems. Budai alone crosses into hyperspace.
- **Sanzu lies exactly between Alpha Odyssey/the Odyssey Expanse and Avici.** It is south of the Expanse, while Avici is farther south on the normal-space pocket's Abyssal boundary. Its stream is not a local stellar wind or an arbitrary directional nebula: it is nebular matter being dragged across light-years toward Avici by the black hole's gravity. The visible flow should therefore orient the journey south toward Avici. Ashen Verge and FOB Ithaca lie one neighboring-system interval directly east of Alpha Odyssey; Last Light lies one interval south and slightly west of Alpha Odyssey; Devoured Reach lies one interval north of Ashen Verge. The current physically advects the player's campaign fleet south unless it is sheltered in the downstream wake of the white dwarf or one of the three planets; this force is additive and never takes directional control away from the player. Most clumps divide around planets, but each clump has a stable 30% chance to ride a planet-passthrough route and wash over the planetary face; the white dwarf always divides the flow.
- Sanzu's campaign haze halves every fleet's sensor range everywhere in the system, including planetary wakes and space beyond the visible river. Shelter affects the current only. The sensor penalty expires after leaving Sanzu and does not add burn or detectability penalties.
- Wall and Ithaca Hammer batteries retain ten front-edge launch ports but now use a narrow 10-degree full fan (+/-5 degrees); the Atropos conversion preserves this same geometry. Magazine, recharge, damage, range and research cycling remain unchanged.
- Every tactical engagement inside Sanzu generates 120 natural nebula terrain patches with softened brightness and thickness. Its 512-by-512 atlas preserves vanilla's exact 4-by-4 pixels and alpha while baking only a cool Sanzu-blue RGB color grade; never runtime-tint it. A separate visual-only `CLOUD_LAYER` pass places 36 randomly rotated 1,600-4,000-unit banks across and slightly beyond the battlefield, providing genuinely broad irregular structure without exposing the terrain tile lattice.
- **Budai is bound to Avici and hyperspace.** Its presence and the black hole's ultramassive, primordial-seeming scale imply that Budai caused Avici to grow into what it is. Preserve this as environmental inference rather than confirming it through an explicit exposition dump.
- Avici's exploration music begins with zippy's non-looping `KARMA_1Loop`, transitions to `KARMA_Loop_A`, then alternates A and `KARMA_Loop_B` for the remainder of the visit. Combat may interrupt the sequence; returning from combat restarts the interrupted phase instead of advancing the playlist in the background.
- Sanzu loops zippy's **Watcher of the Cycle -Scene02- (NoVox)** as its campaign exploration theme. Combat temporarily takes precedence; returning to Sanzu restarts the track and restores the loop.
- Attrition matters: the player should reach Ithaca worn down rather than arrive with an untouched endgame fleet.

#### Gautama

- The supplied composition is the authoritative current visual concept for **Gautama**: [Gautama concept art](assets/scylla_concept_v1.png).
- Gautama is an enormous asymmetrical agglomeration of pale, intricately machined hull masses. Some lobes remain armored and almost architectural; others terminate in stripped ribs, exposed frameworks, dangling machinery, and partially consumed structures.
- Preserve the composition's crowded, many-bodied silhouette and the sense that several incompatible hull organisms have fused into one leviathan when adapting it into combat-ready sprite layers. This reference is not yet collision geometry or a finished game sprite.
- The current implementation uses a transparent combat adaptation of that concept over a dedicated Fabricator-derived base authored at 150% scale. Its sprite pivot, collision geometry, shield, engine emitters, and weapon mounts share the same scaled coordinate system. Gautama uses Energy Lash, carries the Overseer role, and deliberately carries neither the stock `THREAT` hullmod nor `Fragment Swarm`. Before and during Labor V, twenty carrier bays maintain an endlessly renewable screen of custom Attack Swarm wings. Each underlying fighter has only 25% normal hull, takes 150% hull and armor damage, and produces neither a ship-level death explosion nor a hulk; the five-second refit replaces losses without making an individual swarm durable. The one-time post-Labor rematch instead uses an otherwise identical zero-bay skin with no built-in wings or Fabricator escort. Gautama's encounter controller—not a Fabricator ship system—constructs temporary mirrors of the player's deployed ships.

#### Budai

- **Budai** is an enlarged Shrouded Maw surrounded by a permanent spatial distortion. It retains the Maw's smoke and animated body, shifted toward a red-violet palette by adding 36% of each source color's red channel to its blue channel.
- Reuse the visual language of the FOB Ithaca naval shell's impact ripple: a broad luminous refraction that bends the battlefield around the mouth. The effect is maintained rather than pulsed and remains at a radius of 1,000 world units.
- Fire originating outside the current radius deals 70% damage to Budai (30% damage reduction) and produces a brief `DEFLECTED!` impact message. High-explosive hits against its armor or hull are the exception and always deal normal full damage: lore-wise, their detonation expands from inside the distortion. Ships that cross inside retain normal outgoing damage but lose 50% of their translational and turning performance and suffer periodic Shrouded Lens lightning damage.
- Budai itself is subject to the field's movement penalty, has 1.4 times the normal Maw's hull but half its armor, and is rendered and collided at 150% of normal scale. Its built-in Pseudoparticle Manifold makes its two jets four times as wide and 2.5 times as damaging. Its dedicated Hungering Rift missiles are twice normal size and have 1.5 times the normal missile hitpoints. Budai battles open with looping **Heart of Corruption** by Lappy and switch irreversibly to looping **The Savor of Tomorrow** at exactly 50% hull or below during advancing combat. Each cue retries its startup request once, and campaign music resumes after combat. Ordinary Shrouded Dweller encounters do not receive these themes, and ordinary Gautama reincarnations retain Heart of Corruption. It stays in combat at every hull level and has no regeneration mechanic or low-hull system cooldown penalty. Its dedicated system AI adds a 900-unit backward lunge about every six unpaused combat seconds while a hostile non-fighter is within 1,500 units of hull separation and within 60 degrees of its facing. Normal activation, flux, cooldown, and map-edge constraints apply; stock offensive lunges remain available. Normal native Maw smoke continues during enrage, without added density, emission, spread or duration. The red bubble tint is removed; native smoke movement and cleanup remain. On first reaching 50% hull, Budai gives a harsh, inharmonic screech and makes four Convulsive Lunges spaced by his twelve-second cooldown in random directions over independently random distances, taking priority over its ordinary backward lunges. Only after the fourth lunge does the distortion radius quadruple from 1,000 to 4,000 units, and double only its Hungering Rift fire rate for the rest of the battle.
- Maintain one persistent distortion instance tied to the Maw's current position and lifetime; do not spawn a new ripple every frame and allow them to accumulate. Keep its animation frame fixed below GraphicsLib's expiry frame so the field does not pulse.
- Budai's narrative and mechanical home is Avici. On a new campaign it is exactly one vengeance fleet, and both the retired showcase spawner and separate campaign overlay are unable to create a second visible Budai. Existing saves are never cleaned by deleting a serialized duplicate or legacy visual token: a legacy visual becomes inert, and an already-existing duplicate fleet remains the player's save data. A canonical fleet left in hyperspace or another system by an older build is relocated intact to Avici. Budai receives the player's exact position and continuously intercepts them only inside Avici; when the player leaves, it returns to its lair near Kshaya and never follows through a jump point. Avici's black fog neither slows Budai nor breaks its sensor lock. Budai is a one-time encounter: destroying it permanently ends the Avici hunt and it never respawns. The first player-involved defeat queues a one-time rules-authored salvage scene in which Budai disgorges a consumed Onslaught Mk.I. The wreck exists only as a scene visual and is completely cut apart during extraction; it never becomes a recoverable hull or campaign wreck. The operation grants two `chief_navigator_mountable_heavy_adjudicator` weapons with Isa's help, or one without her, and commits `$chief_navigator_budai_heavy_adjudicators_granted_v1`, so repeated callbacks cannot grant it twice. This mountable clone uses the vanilla Heavy Adjudicator's art, projectile, sound, firing behavior, ammo, OP cost, and combat statistics; it removes only the `SYSTEM` hint and `H_A_only` group restriction, and raises base value to 500,000 credits. Its acquisition tags exclude it from blueprints, production, dealers, random drops, and salvage drops. Isa Leicester directs the operation when Hall of Triumph's durable recruitment flag has been observed; otherwise the fleet's salvage chief can extract only one weapon without making Hall of Triumph a dependency.

#### Starving Threat

- The Odyssey includes **Starving Threat**: familiar THREAT hull lineages forced to grow under severe material deprivation.
- They are incomplete rather than conventionally battle-damaged. Whole armor masses and appendages are absent, leaving cleanly terminated ribs, empty sockets, exposed conduits, and underdeveloped machinery.
- Their surviving material is lighter, desaturated, and washed out, with weaker internal glow. Each hull must remain recognizable as a deprived derivative of its original THREAT class.
- The visual language should imply that the encroaching THREAT is exhausting the region's usable matter yet remains capable of fielding dangerous, half-formed warships.
- Starvation has also made the swarms ravenous and unconcerned with stealth. The Starving Threat physiology hullmod raises each affected hull's sensor profile by 200% (three times its normal profile), making fleets much easier to detect while allowing visibility to scale naturally with their composition. Gautama and ordinary THREAT hulls do not receive this modifier.
- Keep their collision geometry deliberately coarse. Use a small hull-bounds polygon around the substantial surviving masses only; do not trace individual ribs, cables, antennae, narrow gaps, empty sockets, or decorative cavities. A projectile that visually clips those fine structures should pass through rather than snag on an over-detailed hitbox.
- Starving hulls retain their normal top speed but have five times their normal acceleration, deceleration, turn acceleration, and turn rate. They have 33% less hull and 15% more armor.
- Every Starving Threat hull carries only the custom **Starving Threat Hull**, which completely replaces stock `Threat Hull`. It inherits the stock combat package but removes the stock zero-profile stealth multiplier, substitutes +200% sensor profile, and suppresses stock reclamation: on death, surviving fragments become attack swarms instead. In ordinary battles, each death has a size-scaled chance to construct one Gautama if the battle has not already contained him: 10% for a frigate, 20% for a destroyer, 30% for a cruiser, and 50% for the Fabricator. Labor V's perimeter fights and the permanent Ungaikyo encounter explicitly suppress this roll. Labor V's center fight permits repeatable cycles: once Gautama is absent, the first eligible death uses its hull-size base chance, each failed roll adds ten percentage points to the next, the total caps at 100%, and a successful construction resets pity. **Heart of Corruption** starts only for an ordinary Gautama reconstruction, while Labor V retains its authored Gospel cue. Combat Chatter is required; a successful ordinary reconstruction flashes `WARNING: Reincarnation`, a blank line, then `Retreat Advised.` once per battle. The final Labor's first successful reconstruction instead flashes `WARNING: Multiple Reincarnations Detected`, once across all cycles. Failed rolls/builds and suppressed encounters show neither reconstruction warning. Each owned Scylla/Siren/Cyclops Strike, Heavenly Strike, Budai, Ungaikyo, Hegemony Expeditionary Fleet, and Persean League Expeditionary Fleet also has its own native smooth opening alert, without changing global faction intros or bypassing Combat Chatter preferences. Detached Attack Swarms fight normally alongside a real deployed hull, but are removed when they become their side's only active units so they never block Starving reserves from entering combat or delay the result.
- A Starving Fabricator constructs only Starving Threat variants. Each construction consumes half the normal amount of the Fabricator's own combat readiness; the spawned unit does not receive an artificial CR penalty.
- Avici contains no THREAT or rogue-derelict fleets; Budai is its only roaming hostile fleet. Ashen Verge maintains ten renewable Ithaca-focused patrols under a dedicated faction mutually hostile to the player and Domain Combat Guard. Their authored assignments remain focused on Ithaca rather than becoming blanket player pursuits. In addition, every 25–35 campaign days a transient First or Second Strike materializes without a source 11,000–15,000 units from Ithaca and immediately attacks one surviving bastion; because the fightable bastion fleet is deliberately hidden on the campaign layer, use Nexerelin's station-assault pattern: `ATTACK_LOCATION` against an invisible token orbiting the bastion, followed by `GO_TO_LOCATION` and `INTERCEPT`, while keeping the real bastion as the tactical priority target. It disappears after a twenty-day attack window if it survives. Outside Labor V, `SpawnSTInvasionFleets` materializes three complete Third Strike fleets in Ashen Verge and permanently assigns one to each bastion. Labor V constructs those same three fleets but holds them at separate standoff tokens for 60 campaign days, preventing campaign autoresolve from bypassing the intended siege window. A survivor then destroys its bastion and attacks its assigned Task Force Spartan formation. TFS roster losses are persistent for the duration of the Labor, and a lane counts as repelled only when its invasion fleet is actually dead. Once all three are destroyed, Gautama's Heavenly Strike enters due west of the Gate Defense Station in Ithaca's western breach. Its thirty-three supporting hulls are staged as three mixed waves; every wave contains one Fabricator, one Hive, and nine assault, line, overseer, and skirmish escorts. It attacks that real station through the same `ATTACK_LOCATION`/`GO_TO_LOCATION`/`INTERCEPT` queue, with the station as its tactical priority, while every surviving TFS formation teleports into a defensive arc around it. The center combat adds two hard-anchored, stock-colored low-tech reserve battlestations; together with the persistent Gate Defense Station, the defenders field three colossal naval lasers rendered from parent-hull slots directly above their intact central foundations. All three station parents inherit Ithaca's completed research upgrades. Command invasions retain a hidden marker solely for battle-side discovery, but every combatant now deals ordinary unmodified damage. The 60-day perimeter window is enforced entirely by the campaign siege timer. Destroying the Heavenly Strike changes the surviving Labor Intel to **Return to Menelaus**, points it at FOB Ithaca, and disables the final-invasion controller without yet completing Labor V. Menelaus's debrief commits completion and queues a separate one-time 480×300 illustrated epilogue interaction after the station dialog closes. Devoured Reach holds twenty-four continuously replaced feeding masses: sixteen press directly against the Devoured Ring and eight orbit and strip the hollow worlds instead of patrolling the system. Its sole Threshold maintains a clear arrival bubble, and no authored Starving Threat fleet may remain on the hyperspace side. The retired eight-swarm Expanse siege is removed from old saves. After Labor V, every authored Starving fleet grows in place to a stable First, Second, or Third Strike role roster, and new replacements spawn at the same tier. Starving fleets use Threat identity rather than Omega identity, expose no human patrol comm channel, and migrate any serialized Omega commander presentation in place without replacing the officer or fleet. Every THREAT fleet is marked unable to use jump points and any THREAT fleet found in hyperspace is removed or, for Gautama, returned to the Devoured Ring. Gautama is mechanically distinct from the Starving hulls and does not use either Starving Threat or stock `THREAT` hullmods.

- Campaign autoresolve does not run combat damage listeners. If it eliminates a command-spawned invasion before the player participates, that lane re-forms and resumes its attack. After the player joins a lane's battle, replacement is disabled there, preserving the player's attrition and making the resulting victory permanent.
- The three owned Ithaca bastions repair surviving hulls/armor/modules proportionally over 30 campaign days; destroyed stations and their click proxies rebuild after 30 eligible days. Preserve live tokens/members rather than replacing them, and skip paused, busy, foreign/player/incompatible state. Active Labor V suspends all timed repairs, and its failure blocks them permanently. Its explicit opening fully restores every bastion before committing fleets or siege clocks; no missing, never-deployed lane can be credited as repelled. Routine loads never reset saved invasion victories.
- Command-spawned invasion fleets are exclusive siege actors: each continuously restores its assigned bastion as both tactical priority and active target, ignores the player, TFS fleets, the other bastions, and unrelated fleets, and receives reciprocal non-interception exclusions from NPC fleets. Faction hostility remains unchanged, allowing the player to voluntarily join the bastion battle without becoming the invasion's campaign-map distraction.

### Waypoint Troy and the Odyssey Expanse prototype

Each neighboring leg uses approximately the same hyperspace spacing as Alpha Odyssey-to-Sanzu and Sanzu-to-Avici, keeping all six destinations readable within one compact pocket. Last Light is a sparse white-dwarf system containing the locked Guardian wreck and, after Labor V, Ungaikyo's one-time Third Strike. Devoured Reach is a starless sixth system containing three barren worlds with continental-scale holes eaten completely through them. Those hollow worlds remain rendered throughout the system regardless of sensor distance. All six Odyssey systems have permanent canonical hyperspace anchors so their system markers remain visible throughout the pocket.
- The Devoured Ring and three hollow worlds use dedicated transparent campaign art in [`graphics/campaign/devoured_reach`](../graphics/campaign/devoured_reach). Although Avici itself straddles the normal-to-Abyss transition, all three of its hyperspace jump points sit on a broad inward-facing arc wholly inside the pocket's zero-depth terrain. Aggressively delete untracked procgen jump points, gravity wells, and nascent wells inside the reserved cavity while preserving the Foxtrot Terminus Aperture and the sixteen authored system jump points.

Existing saves migrate its historical campaign fleet out of Avici. Menelaus's debrief reveals it was a local coalescence of **Ungaikyo**, a web of nanometer-scale black filaments spanning light-years through Devoured Reach. Afterward, the historical fleet token moves intact to Last Light and coils around the derelict Guardian above Ember. A third-party source that throws during construction is cleaned up and permanently stops regenerating; it is never replaced by an unrelated fallback, and other slots continue normally. Pending and live reflections collapse when Ungaikyo dies, Gautama reconstruction stays suppressed, the Guardian unlocks for normal recovery, and the patrol never returns after that permanent defeat. Starving Hive Units use the exact native twelve-weapon fit and geometry, fragment reserve/regeneration bonuses, Fragment Volley and timid support doctrine in both campaign and fabricated deployments; shared Starving depletion/death traits and other roles remain unchanged. Budai receives the player's exact position directly rather than through campaign sensors, but only while both fleets are inside Avici. Existing saves resume at the first unfinished Labor in the new order based on their durable completion flags; stale unfinished Intel entries retire without being marked complete.

### Arrival and climax

- The expedition's main strategic objective is to **defeat the THREAT Hive**.
- Progression toward that confrontation is driven by exploration: the player searches the surrounding region for unique station components that permanently expand Ithaca's defenses.
- Menelaus uses the recovered blueprints to unlock technology the player can use to refit and prepare their fleet for the Hive battle. These discoveries form the central preparation loop, not optional side rewards.
- Ithaca initially appears abandoned and silent.
- **Menelaus**, an AI core, manages FOB Ithaca and commands its defenses.
- The player's initial battle at Ithaca is against a force of visually recolored Fourteenth Battlegroup ships associated with the **Spartan Division**.
- During the approach and battle, these ships are presented so that the player naturally assumes they are automated warships under Menelaus's direct control.
- After the player breaks through the outer defenses, that assumption is overturned: the Spartan Division ships are crewed by human Fourteenth Battlegroup naval personnel who are initially presented as survivors thawed from cryogenic storage.
- The reveal distinguishes Menelaus's role as Ithaca's managing intelligence from the human agency and continuity embodied by the awakened Spartan crews.
- The awakened crews call themselves **Domain soldiers**. They do not identify as Hegemony personnel merely because the Hegemony descended from the stranded Fourteenth Battlegroup.
- Sanzu remains free of hostile Guard patrols, Starving Threat, and Budai, but it is not uncontested. Completing Labor I causes one large, personally hostile fleet called **Persean League Survivors** to arrive in the system. Its battered formation does not respawn after defeat and is centered on a Pegasus supported by a Champion, Eagles, missile and line cruisers, destroyers, and frigates. Every hull carries D-mods and damaged combat readiness; the relatively intact Pegasus keeps the formation dangerous despite its attrition. Fighting it does not alter ordinary Persean League reputation. Sanzu also attracts no more than two small, non-hostile Persean exploration patrols at once, each consisting of one or two escorts and one civilian survey or logistics hull. Only after Oren Voss's main Hegemony expedition arrives can up to two battered Hegemony exile patrols appear in Sanzu. Voss appropriated their supplies and ordered them onto suicide patrols; they defied him and fled. Each exile group reassesses the player every four to nine days, with a 45% chance to turn hostile and engage and otherwise actively avoiding the player. Their ships carry one or two D-mods and reduced combat readiness. Both patrol populations remain confined to Sanzu and replace losses only after a randomized 18-32 day interval.
- Completing Labor III causes a battered Hegemony expedition to reach Alpha Odyssey and establish a permanent forward base there. The arrival requires completion of all three preceding milestones: the four Guard Motherships, four Ithaca defense components delivered and installed, and the Drifting Wall. Nearly every ship carries multiple D-mods. Most of its surviving column consists of fuel tenders, freighters, and other logistics hulls; its combat strength is concentrated in many frigates, several Enforcers, a few cruisers, and one Legion XIV. The Legion is itself scarred but has the maximum three S-mods, the expedition's one lovingly maintained instrument of force. Desperation has converted emergency authority into piracy with paperwork: Admiral Oren Voss hunts the player and demands supplies and fuel "for the good of humanity," attacking if the requisition is refused or cannot be met. He arbitrarily recalculates supply rations, confiscates private stores as collective property, and sends malcontents to dangerous, barren reaches of the Orion Knot. Their meager returns and predictable failures become further charges against them until he can justify exile. Voss is a communist satire/critique whose constant invocation of the **Good of humanity** becomes more grandiose as his grip on reality deteriorates. This personal hostility is isolated from the wider Hegemony reputation system, but his forward base, patrols, and recurring requisitions make it difficult for the player to leave the Orion Knot unmolested.
- After the expedition arrives, Voss naively invokes the Fourteenth Battlegroup's supposed continuity and attempts to take command of the entire FOB Ithaca macrocomplex. Menelaus refuses and cites his standing authority as **Strategos**: the Hegemony is not the Domain and possesses no authority over Ithaca. Task Force Spartan explicitly recognizes Menelaus as Ithaca's commander and aligns with him. Humiliated by that rejection, Voss calls Menelaus a machine, a core, and merely a tool incapable of rank or humanity. Menelaus answers in a furious burst that saturates the entire command circuit: **"I AM HUMAN!"** He casts Voss out of Ithaca's command channel. The expedition makes no attempt to cross Spartan's firing line afterward, but remains at its Alpha Odyssey forward base and turns to armed requisitions and harassment of the Orion Knot approaches.
- They are effectively living fossils: people of the Domain preserved intact, carrying its military identity, assumptions, loyalties, and institutional culture into a Sector where their civilization has been dead for centuries.

#### Task Force Spartan, the breach, and its legacy

- Early in the Odyssey, before the route's repetition is fully apparent, the expedition encounters an isolated fragment of Ithaca's wall drifting far from the macrobase. The fragment is large enough to be mistaken for a complete station. The rebuilt encounter begins with one enormous, horizontally mirrored foundation whose two exposed ends are identically broken, charred, and torn open.
- The foundation is the non-destroyable Vast Bulk parent of a modular station. It is assembled from one damaged left-half sprite mirrored across a clean central join, giving the fight a broad, symmetrical base without independently drifting artwork. Future weapons and functional structures attach as destructible child station modules; defeating those children, not damaging the foundation, supplies the victory condition. The old seven-berth curved frame is a legacy prototype rather than the new fight's geometry.
- The fragment's origin is not explained at the time. When the player eventually reaches Ithaca, its architecture matches an enormous hole torn through the fortress wall.
- Two mega-turrets survive on each side of that breach. **Task Force Spartan (TFS)** occupies the open center as a mobile defensive line, plugging the hole against the encroaching THREAT.
- Menelaus has committed TFS to this weakest sector as part of Ithaca's last stand. Menelaus and the three fixed Spartan guard fleets belong to one hidden Task Force Spartan faction. Its relationship with both Starving Threat factions is fixed at hostile, while its relationship with the player is fixed at cooperative, allowing Spartan to enter nearby player-versus-Threat battles. Spartan is not embedded into the wall or replacing its modules; the fleet is physically holding the gap between the surviving wall shoulders.
- Task Force Spartan's standard is the austere muted-silver spear-and-broken-ring device hanging behind Sinni in her Eventide office. That mark is TFS's own faction logo and crest, not Hegemony heraldry.
- The current standalone TFS combat test begins with one XIV Onslaught and two XIV Dominators. Its visible, transponder-on campaign fleet is continuously reconciled in the Odyssey Expanse: older fleet objects are relocated intact, accidental losses are restored outside combat, and a missing fleet is recreated regardless of its historical one-shot spawn marker. Sixty seconds after engagement, a leading Eagle XIV and two flanking Lashers enter through the combat map's bottom border. They deploy from the edge and fly into the battle rather than teleporting into position behind the player's ship.
- The player may win or lose the confrontation with TFS. Either result ultimately de-escalates into a peaceful resolution: the battle establishes the relationship and reveals the people behind the Domain warships, but neither outcome requires the destruction of Task Force Spartan or leaves the expedition and TFS as permanent enemies.
- Near the end of the journey, the TFS commander reveals that every member of Task Force Spartan is a **flash clone**. The original Spartan personnel perished decades ago; the people encountered by the expedition are their deliberately created successors, not the cryogenically preserved originals they appeared to be.
- The commander sacrifices himself toward the end of the journey, ensuring that the expedition and the surviving members of TFS can continue.
- After the Odyssey, the surviving Spartans take up places within the Hegemony rather than attempting to preserve TFS indefinitely as an isolated Domain formation.
- Some retire and begin families with the limited time remaining to them. Many become instructors, determined to leave behind their knowledge, standards, and a living institutional legacy before they die.
- The flash-clone revelation does not make their chosen identity meaningless: their ending is defined by what they pass on, not by whether they were the original bodies.

- Entering its perimeter triggers klaxons and automated reactivation because another THREAT wave is arriving—not because the player tripped a generic dungeon trap.
- The surrounding Domain Explorarium derelicts awaken and engage THREAT.
- These restored teal Explorarium combat hulls identify as the **Domain Combat Guard**, a surviving autonomous subfaction of the Domain Explorarium rather than ordinary green derelicts.
- The player's objective is to **make the derelicts win**.
- Explorarium drones remain consistent with lore: they are deliberately limited post-THREAT autonomous warships, not secretly superior superweapons.
- The derelicts provide mass, screening, delaying actions, and objective control; the player's fleet provides quality, judgment, and decisive intervention.
- Success should care about how much of Ithaca's irreplaceable defense reserve survives, not only whether the final hostile ship dies.
- The shelved Omega prototype is not part of the Odyssey's active narrative. The quest's personal revelation instead concerns Helen's surviving record, Menelaus's love for her, and his manipulation of her departure from Ithaca.

### Fortress battle map idea — not implemented

- Most encounters within **FOB Ithaca** take place on fortress-style combat maps; this is Ithaca's primary battle format rather than a one-off set piece.
- The three intact cardinal sections of Ithaca's orbital ring become separate north, east, and south bastion assaults selected from the fixed campaign station. Each section fields a fully operational seven-emplacement wall and a different formation of pale-teal Domain Explorarium drones. Its exposed cyan section core is recessed behind the gun line. A single smooth connected rear bulkhead spans the entire hostile map edge with physical collision, so the player cannot circle behind the installation and bypass its defenses to reach the core.
- Include **phase boarding ships** as an encounter and assault concept: specialized vessels that use phase capability to deliver boarding forces through or around conventional fortress defenses.
- Their exact boarding rules, valid targets, counters, and whether the player can command or merely oppose them remain open design questions.
- Each fortress map uses automatically generated turret placements, allowing different sections of the macrobase to produce distinct defensive layouts.
- Include a dedicated fortress-style combat map built around enormous fixed defenses.
- The fortress has gigantic turrets, each comparable in scale to a full ship rather than a normal station weapon mount.
- These turrets can be captured during the battle and turned against the opposing force.
- After capturing one, the player can press **X** to transfer direct control into the turret, using the same basic command-transfer language as switching between ships.
- The player should be able to alternate between piloting a fleet ship and personally operating captured fortress weapons as the battle changes.
- Exact capture rules, turret loadouts, AI behavior, map layout, and transfer implementation remain open design questions.
- The fortress-map prototype uses a second, darkened wall layer as a **mounting wall** extending from the foreground barrier beneath the gun line. It renders below all ships and has no collision or targeting marker, so the Onslaught-scale turret ships read as emplacements physically seated in Ithaca. Keep this visual mounting layer distinct from the foreground wall's collision geometry.
- The placeholder Onslaught emplacements are replaced by invisible, fixed, single-slot support ships. Each slot carries one fortress-scale naval gun derived from the Mark IX autocannon: an Onslaught-sized, long-barreled weapon colored to match Ithaca's wall. Its frigate-scale shells inflict 100,000 kinetic damage per hit.

## Intended emotional arc

1. Temptation: a credible promise of immense Domain treasure.
2. Commitment: surrender normal navigational control and assemble a constrained expedition.
3. Ordeal: surf an impossible route while supplies and combat readiness erode.
4. False relief: reach the colossal, apparently dead FOB Ithaca.
5. Awakening: the macrobase and debris field reactivate under cold automated protocols.
6. Siege: help purposefully constrained Domain machines defeat the older nightmare they were constrained because of.
7. Revelation: learn that Helen was Sinni's Task Force Spartan ancestor, that Menelaus loved her, and that he manipulated her departure with the Fourteenth so she would survive.
8. Reckoning: confront what Menelaus preserved, what he abandoned, and what Helen's record brought her descendant back to find.
9. Consequence: Sinni completes Helen's route in reverse while Ithaca's surviving reserve records how well the player saved it.

## Design boundaries

- Do not portray Troy's staging anchorage as a Domain macrobase or let it replace Ithaca as the destination.
- Do not reduce the crossing to a single scripted teleport or ordinary travel leg.
- Do not make Sinni incompetent or default to a predictable smuggler betrayal.
- Do not portray Explorarium drones as stronger than THREAT in direct technological terms.
- Do not make allied derelicts disposable set dressing; their survival is the core battle objective.
- Do not make Ithaca fully functional or able to win without the player.
- Do not let the climax become a normal fleet wipe with no persistent cost to Ithaca's reserve.

## Open implementation questions

These were not firmly settled and should remain open until technical prototyping:

- How the temporary Troy departure waypoint is selected and signaled.
- Whether the expedition uses hard fleet limits, soft logistical pressure, or both.
- How much control Sinni temporarily takes from the player during traversal.
- Whether encounters happen on the campaign map, as custom battle scenarios, or in a hybrid sequence.
- How surviving named or designated derelicts persist after the defense.
- Whether Ithaca supports later repeat attacks, exploration, recovery, or a return journey.
- Exact rewards, failure states, and compatibility requirements for THREAT and Shrouded Dwellers content.

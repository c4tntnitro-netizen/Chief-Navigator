## Starsector sprite orientation — recurring pitfall

- Do **not** assume that the top of a PNG will render toward the top of the combat map when a ship or station core has `facing = 0`.
- Starsector treats the top of conventional hull art as the hull's forward direction, while combat facing `0°` points east. An upright source sprite therefore appears rotated by 90° at `facing = 0`.
- For art authored with its forward edge at the top of the PNG: use `90°` to face north, `270°` to face south, `0°` to face east, and `180°` to face west.
- A `.ship` center is measured from the sprite's bottom-left, not its top-left. For an upright hull, map local engine coordinates to PNG pixels with `pixelX = centerX - localY` and `pixelY = height - centerY - localX`. Gautama's engine origins are authored against the painted rear silhouette of `chief_navigator_scylla_v2.png`, not the scaled vanilla Fabricator's empty-margin positions. All three Gautama skins inherit the base engine geometry; retain the fourteen engines and their angles, flare sizes, style, weapons, center, and other hull geometry. Check `tools/validate_gautama_engines.ps1`; do not infer engine placement from the older hardpoint validator's top-origin Y assumption.
- This has caused multiple Chief Navigator station and wall sprites to be laid out 90° wrong. Treat it as a recurring integration hazard, not an isolated mistake.
- Before tuning module spacing, arcs, collision bounds, or weapon offsets, first verify the rendered cardinal orientation in combat with one asymmetric module. Do not compensate for a 90° orientation error by moving or rotating every child slot.
- For a horizontal station-core layout whose module centers are already correct, rotate the `STATION_MODULE` slot angles rather than rotating the parent core, because changing the parent also rotates the attachment layout. GDI+ frame composition and Starsector child-module facing apply opposite signs to the visual curvature. If the parent-frame berth curves are `9, 6, 3, 0, -3, -6, -9` around a 180-degree visual baseline, use the inverse curvature around the insert's `270°` slot baseline: `261, 264, 267, 270, 273, 276, 279`. Reusing the frame's curvature sign produces per-module errors of approximately `-18, -12, -6, 0, +6, +12, +18` degrees; it is not a uniform offset.
## Modular ship and station design rules

- Treat every station or ship module as a separate ship attached to a controllable parent. Each module has its own armor grid, flux pool, ship system, hullmods, and combat state. Use this separation to create progressive destruction and local defenses rather than inflating one hull's global statistics.
- Avoid extreme armor (especially above roughly 2,000) because Starsector's armor calculation can make low-damage weapons functionally irrelevant. Avoid extreme flux/dissipation because percentage-based hard-flux dissipation can make a boss impossible to meaningfully pressure. Prefer several reasonably tuned destroyable modules.
- Modular defenses can easily become overtuned. Give the encounter explicit weaknesses: vulnerable gaps, a comparatively fragile parent, arcs that can be flanked, or modules whose loss materially reduces the boss. Players should feel the station weaken as sections are destroyed.
- Players cannot directly control ordinary ship modules. Keep enough meaningful weapons or systems on a player-controllable parent; otherwise piloting it becomes passive. This matters less for NPC-only FOB Ithaca structures, but remains a useful design check.
- Enemy AI counts modules as separate ships. A capital parent with five capital-class modules may be evaluated as six capital ships and discourage engagement. Unless a specific behavior requires otherwise, give attached modules a small hull size (generally frigate) and consider the station-PD-drone treatment for non-primary defensive plates so AI threat assessment remains sane.
- Hostile AI tends to aim at ship/module center points. Armor-module center offsets can manipulate targeting toward the parent's center and expose intentional gaps, but they distort some UI previews and should not be a default for weapon or functional modules. Prefer a correctly centered sprite plus `moduleAnchor` for ordinary placement; use center-offset targeting tricks only deliberately.
- `always_detaches` should be the normal destruction behavior so a killed section breaks away and opens the parent. `never_detaches` leaves a dead module as a large obstruction and can turn fights into slogs; use it only when the resulting obstruction has exploitable gaps and is intentionally part of the encounter.
- Every module requires an entry in `ship_data.csv`, its own `.ship`, and its own `.variant`. The parent needs the `SHIP_WITH_MODULES` hint. Module hulls should normally be `UNBOARDABLE` and `HIDE_IN_CODEX`; use `DO_NOT_SHOW_MODULES_IN_FLEET_LIST` when they must also disappear from fleet/refit selectors. A module with no OP is effectively unselectable even without a tag.
- Useful module hints/tags: `UNDER_PARENT` renders the module below its parent; `module_hull_bar_only` hides module flux UI while retaining its hull bar; `module_no_status_bar` hides all mouseover/lock-on status and prevents selection; `module_unselectable` blocks refit customization; pair `module_refit_bright` with it when the module should remain normally lit in refit.
- Mount modules on `STATION_MODULE` slots. Placement is the relationship between the slot location and the child sprite's center, or preferably the child's `moduleAnchor` when the visual attachment point differs from its image center. Keep ordinary module sprite centers at the image center so previews remain correct.
- Variant module order is render order: modules later/lower in the parent `.variant` render above earlier modules. Parent-hull weapons render above ordinary ship modules and below fighter modules. Use `UNDER_PARENT` and variant order intentionally instead of compensating with duplicate background sprites.
- Useful module hullmods: `axialrotation` rotates about the module axis; `anchorrotation` rotates about its attachment anchor; `shield_always_on` can combine with the latter for rotating shield emitters; `reduced_explosion` protects the parent from module death blasts; `vastbulk` keeps a parent alive until its modules die; `do_not_fire_through` discourages other modules firing through it; `shared_flux_sink` redistributes its contribution across surviving modules.
- Officer skills affect a parent and its attached modules, while hullmods remain per-module. Account for both when balancing the station rather than evaluating the raw CSV stats in isolation.
